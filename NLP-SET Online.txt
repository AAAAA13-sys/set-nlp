# Chapter I: The Problem and Its Background

## Introduction

In higher education institutions, the Student Evaluation of Teachers (SET) functions as an administrative mechanism for measuring pedagogical effectiveness, determining faculty tenure or promotion, and driving continuous curriculum refinement. Historically executed using manual paper questionnaires, these evaluations were plagued by administrative burdens, including high printing costs, slow physical distribution, manual optical mark recognition (OMR) tabulation bottlenecks, and transcription errors. To modernize this process, educational institutions transitioned toward web-based evaluation platforms integrated into student information systems.

Despite digitizing the survey instruments, online evaluation periods remain fraught with structural, behavioral, and technological vulnerabilities. In most university models, the evaluation period is restricted to a narrow, high-stakes operational window (typically 7 to 14 days preceding midterm or final examinations). To achieve viable institutional response rates, academic administrations frequently impose mandatory administrative hurdles, such as gating final grade releases or digital examination permits behind evaluation completion.

Mandatory gating triggers acute concurrency spikes on institutional servers as thousands of enrolled students attempt to complete evaluations simultaneously. The resulting traffic surges frequently trigger server timeouts, lost submissions, and database locking deadlocks. Furthermore, coercive completion requirements induce survey fatigue, prompting students to resort to "speed-running"—submitting identical Likert-scale ratings (e.g., straight 5s or straight 1s) and low-effort or blank qualitative comments within seconds merely to unlock examination clearances. Concurrently, students often fear potential grade retaliation from instructors, leading to inflated, inauthentic scores if platform anonymity is perceived as compromised. At the administrative level, Deans and Department Chairs receive consolidated evaluation reports weeks or months after academic terms have concluded, rendering the collected feedback reactive rather than proactive.

To address these vulnerabilities, this study develops and evaluates **EvaluaTech**, an automated Student Evaluation of Teachers (SET) platform equipped with integrity-guarded completion queues, double-blind cryptographic clearance synchronization, response authenticity validation, and automated sentiment-driven feedback analytics. By decoupling student clearance tokens from anonymized survey payloads, enforcing interaction timers, and utilizing Natural Language Processing (NLP) to parse unstructured student feedback, the platform transforms the evaluation season into an authentic, secure, and immediate feedback ecosystem.

## Review of Related Literature

### Software Architecture and Concurrency in Educational Web Platforms

The implementation of institutional evaluation systems requires architectural patterns that handle bursty, high-concurrency traffic while preserving transactional integrity. Web applications relying on standard relational architectures often suffer database contention and deadlocks when high volumes of students write evaluation records simultaneously.

Peter and Mulyawan demonstrated that multi-tier web platforms utilizing secure relational databases, server-side data validation, and strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) provide the transaction isolation necessary to prevent data overwrites and maintain operational integrity. Hamadi emphasized that iterative and modular architectures allow systems to scale administrative workflows and isolate data updates without disrupting frontend access. When handling financial, grading, or clearance states, Indumathi et al. identified that robust backend data pipelines must enforce atomic transactions to prevent double-submissions or orphaned tracking logs during high network loads.

### Survey Gating, Evaluation Fatigue, and Response Quality

Linking institutional requirements (such as viewing examination permits or final term marks) to survey completion is an established method to boost participation rates. However, research highlights significant behavioral trade-offs. Goos and Salomons demonstrated that mandatory evaluation policies increase nominal completion numbers but elevate measurement error caused by student disengagement. When completing surveys under coercion, students exhibit satisficing behaviors, including uniform rating selections across distinct teaching dimensions (straight-lining) and rapid clicking to bypass forms.

Spooren et al. surveyed methodological validity in student evaluations, highlighting that non-differentiated responses heavily bias faculty appraisals and distort tenure benchmarks. Addressing satisficing requires real-time algorithmic checks, including page dwell-time validation, inter-item variance calculations, and front-end interaction throttling, which encourage authentic engagement without deadlocking the user's progress.

### Cryptographic Anonymity and Retaliation Mitigation

Perceptions of compromised anonymity significantly suppress critical, honest student feedback. Stroebe observed that students routinely give inflated evaluations to instructors whom they perceive as punitive or demanding if they suspect that negative feedback can be traced back to their student profiles before grades are submitted.

To overcome the tension between verifying individual student clearance and maintaining survey anonymity, zero-knowledge architectural decoupling is required. Modern transaction platforms implement dual-token architectures: one token securely records student clearance status against institutional rosters, while an unlinked, salted cryptographic hash records the survey responses without identifying headers. This decoupling guarantees that administrators can monitor evaluation compliance without compromising student identities.

### Natural Language Processing and Sentiment Analytics in Course Feedback

While Likert-scale responses provide standardized numerical metrics, open-ended narrative comments contain nuanced contextual diagnostic information regarding instructional pacing, syllabus coverage, and classroom decorum. However, manually categorizing thousands of free-text student comments across dozens of academic departments is practically impossible for academic deans before the start of subsequent semesters.

Kastrati et al. established that Natural Language Processing (NLP) and sentiment classification algorithms—such as the Valence Aware Dictionary and sEntiment Reasoner (VADER) and Transformer-based models (e.g., RoBERTa)—can accurately classify short, context-heavy educational text into distinct polarity bands (positive, neutral, negative). In parallel, text-clustering algorithms can categorize feedback into operational pedagogical domains, allowing academic chairs to quickly isolate systemic issues without reading raw, uncurated data dumps.

### Usability and Software Quality Frameworks

Evaluating interactive enterprise systems requires both functional verification and formal usability assessments. Deshmukh and Chalmeta validated the System Usability Scale (SUS) as a dependable metric for quantifying user satisfaction and cognitive friction across digital interfaces. Usability scores above 68 reflect above-average platform usability, while scores exceeding 80 denote institutional-grade systems.

To evaluate overall software quality, Ratumbuisang and Ratumbuisang and Falco and Robiolo utilized the international standard ISO/IEC 25010. This standard evaluates software systems across core quality characteristics: Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Usability, Reliability, and Portability. Applying ISO/IEC 25010 alongside automated functional testing ensures that enterprise applications meet both technical and user-centric benchmarks before deployment.

## Statement of the Problem

This study addresses the operational bottlenecks, data degradation, and security vulnerabilities that occur during institutional Student Evaluation of Teachers (SET) seasons by answering the following research questions:

1. How can a web-based evaluation platform be architected to enable secure, high-concurrency evaluation queuing, instantaneous cryptographic anonymity decoupling, and atomic examination clearance issuance upon survey completion?
2. To what extent does the integration of real-time response authenticity validation (speed-run detection and zero-variance filtering) alongside NLP-driven sentiment analytics improve the diagnostic utility and reliability of student feedback for academic deans and department chairs?
3. What is the level of software quality, operational efficiency, and user acceptability of the proposed system, as evaluated through ISO/IEC 25010 quality characteristics, the System Usability Scale (SUS), and automated concurrency transaction benchmarks?

## Objectives of the Study

### General Objective

To design, develop, and evaluate **EvaluaTech**, a web-based Student Evaluation of Teachers (SET) platform that provides integrity-guarded evaluation queues, automated clearance synchronization, response authenticity checks, and sentiment-driven instructional feedback analytics for higher education institutions.

### Specific Objectives

1. To engineer a secure, role-based platform that imports academic course-section rosters, dynamically populates individual student evaluation queues, and executes decoupled, atomic transactions that grant examination clearance while anonymizing survey responses.
2. To implement data-integrity mechanisms that detect rushed or uniform submissions (straight-lining) and deploy a Python-based NLP pipeline that categorizes open-ended student feedback by sentiment polarity and pedagogical theme.
3. To construct role-tailored dashboards for Deans, Department Chairs, and Faculty that visualize real-time completion rates, Likert-scale diagnostic summaries, and sentiment-classified feedback trends.

## Operational Definition of Terms

* **Student Evaluation of Teachers (SET):** The institutional survey process wherein enrolled students assess faculty instructional performance across predefined pedagogical dimensions.
* **Evaluation Queue:** A personalized list of pending faculty evaluations generated dynamically for a student based on their active course enrollment.
* **Clearance Token:** A digital credential issued by the system upon completion of all assigned evaluations, updating the student's institutional record to grant access to examination permits or grade sheets.
* **Cryptographic Decoupling:** The technical separation of the student's identifying metadata from their submitted survey responses using one-way salted hashes and isolated transaction tables.
* **Mandatory Timing Policy & Response Authenticity:** An institutional policy and validation mechanism that enables students to submit completed evaluations whenever they are ready without individual minimum waiting periods or maximum answering durations, within administrator-defined evaluation windows.
* **Straight-Lining (Zero Variance):** A satisficing response pattern where a respondent selects identical Likert ratings across all survey items regardless of question phrasing.
* **Sentiment Polarity Score:** A metric generated by Natural Language Processing ranging from $-1.0$ (strongly negative) to $+1.0$ (strongly positive) quantifying the emotional valence of open-ended comments.
* **Double-Blind Viewing Lock:** A role-based access guard that prevents faculty members from viewing aggregated evaluation results until the registrar has officially closed and locked term grade submissions.

## Scope and Limitations of the Study

### Scope of the Study

The platform is designed for deployment in collegiate academic environments. The functional scope encompasses:

1. **Role-Based Workspaces:** Dedicated interfaces for System Administrators, Deans, Department Chairs, Faculty, and Students.


2. **Academic Catalog & Roster Ingestion:** Ingestion of CSV/JSON course enrollments linking students, instructors, and subject codes.
3. **Queue & Anonymized Submission Engine:** Dynamic student-specific evaluation queues, atomic clearance token generation, and decoupled survey storage.
4. **Data Integrity Guards:** Administrator-controlled evaluation windows and population variance calculation guards to identify uniform response patterns without imposing artificial waiting periods.
5. **Sentiment & Pedagogical Analytics:** NLP-based text classification of narrative comments into positive, neutral, and negative sentiment, cross-referenced with criteria including *Pedagogical Delivery*, *Assessment Fairness*, and *Classroom Decorum*.
6. **Reporting Engine:** Department-level completion heatmaps, instructor score distribution cards, and ISO/IEC 25010 / SUS evaluation instrumentation.



### Limitations of the Study

1. **Authentication Scope:** System authentication relies on session-based credentials and institutional email verification; direct integration with proprietary enterprise Single Sign-On (e.g., Active Directory / Shibboleth) is simulated via standard OAuth/LDAP adapters.


2. **Language Constraints:** The NLP sentiment engine is optimized for English and standard institutional vernacular; multilingual dialect processing (such as informal local slang or code-switching) is not natively modeled in the baseline dictionary.
3. **Historical Data Dependencies:** Advanced multi-term longitudinal trending requires historical evaluation records; new installations will fall back to single-term statistical benchmarks.
4. **Policy Independence:** The system automates clearance issuance based on software rules but does not set academic policy regarding mandatory evaluation compliance or tenure criteria.

# Chapter II: Methodology

## Architectural Framework

EvaluaTech is engineered using a decoupled, four-tier web architecture that isolates presentation, business workflows, analytical computation, and relational persistence:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        Presentation Tier                               │
│      Responsive Web Interfaces (Blade, Modern JavaScript, CSS3)        │
│   [Student Queue]    [Faculty Portal]    [Dean/Chair Dashboard]       │
└───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┘
                                    │ HTTPS / RESTful JSON
┌───────────────────────────────────▼────────────────────────────────────┐
│                       Application Logic Tier                           │
│                      (Laravel Framework / PHP)                         │
│  - Authentication & RBAC Guards        - Concurrency & Idempotency     │
│  - Enrollment & Queue Generator         - Double-Blind Visibility Guard │
│  - Decoupled Clearance Engine           - Data-Integrity Validators    │
└──────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────┘
                   │                                 │
                   │ Background Worker API           │ SQL Queries (ACID)
┌──────────────────▼──────────────┐   ┌──────────────▼───────────────────┐
│         Analytics Tier          │   │        Persistence Tier          │
│         (Python / Flask)        │   │          (MySQL Schema)          │
│  - VADER / TextBlob NLP Engine  │   │  - Users & Enrollments Table     │
│  - Feedback Topic Clustering    │   │  - Cryptographic Clearance Store │
│  - Sentiment Polarity Scoring   │   │  - Anonymized Evaluation Vault   │
└─────────────────────────────────┘   └──────────────────────────────────┘

```

1. **Presentation Tier:** Built using responsive HTML5, modern CSS frameworks, and lightweight client-side JavaScript, ensuring consistent rendering across mobile viewports ($375\times812$ px) and desktop administrative terminals ($1366\times768$ px).


2. **Application Logic Tier (Laravel/PHP):** Governs core transaction execution, role authorization, enrollment mapping, submission idempotency, and the double-blind visibility state machine.


3. **Analytics Tier (Python Microservice):** Executes asynchronous text analytics via Flask, using VADER and Scikit-Learn pipelines to calculate comment sentiment polarity and cluster keyword frequencies without blocking backend web requests.


4. **Persistence Tier (MySQL 8.0 Engine):** An InnoDB database configured with strict foreign-key integrity constraints, row-level pessimistic locking (`SELECT ... FOR UPDATE`), and atomic transaction isolation.

### 1. Dual-Token Cryptographic Decoupling Workflow

To resolve the contradiction between tracking clearance and preserving anonymity:

* The system instantiates two distinct database models: `EvaluationClearance` and `EvaluationVault`.
* When an evaluation is submitted, the transaction updates the student's row in `EvaluationClearance` to mark the subject complete.
* Simultaneously, the survey answers are stripped of headers, IP addresses, and user IDs, salted with an application secret, and written to `EvaluationVault`. No foreign key exists between these tables, rendering retrospective tracing computationally infeasible while maintaining full auditability of clearance compliance.



### 2. Double-Blind Faculty Access Control

To protect students from grade retribution:

* Aggregated faculty score reports are locked behind a database guard:

$$\text{CanViewEvaluation} = (\text{TermGradesSubmitted} = \text{TRUE}) \land (\text{RegistrarLock} = \text{TRUE})$$


* Instructors cannot view quantitative ratings, distribution charts, or student sentiment summaries until their submitted grades for that course section are officially verified and locked by the registrar.



### 3. Data Integrity & Mandatory Timing Policy

To ensure high-quality and unmanipulated responses, the evaluation engine enforces two operational mechanisms:

* **Mandatory Timing Policy & Immediate Submission:** In accordance with institutional accessibility standards, students are permitted to submit completed evaluations whenever they are ready without an artificial minimum waiting duration or maximum answering limit, provided submissions occur within the administrator-controlled active evaluation window.
* **Straight-Line Variance Check:** The backend calculates the variance ($\sigma^2$) across all Likert items. If $\sigma^2 = 0$ (e.g., every question is marked 5 or 1), the platform prompts the user to confirm their uniform assessment, logging an integrity warning flag in administrative aggregate reporting.

## Software Engineering Methodology

The development of EvaluaTech is governed by the **Agile Development Methodology**, structured across continuous sprint iterations:

1. **Sprint 1 (Architecture & Role Ingestion):** Schema definition, database seeding of student/faculty rosters, and Role-Based Access Control (RBAC).


2. **Sprint 2 (Queue & Clearance Engine):** Implementation of dynamic student queues, transactional submission handling, and clearance issuance.


3. **Sprint 3 (Integrity & Concurrency Safeguards):** Engineering submission dwell-time validation, straight-line detection, row-level pessimistic locking, and idempotency key checks.


4. **Sprint 4 (Analytics & Sentiment Pipeline):** Integration of the Flask/Python NLP engine for sentiment polarity scoring and text categorization.


5. **Sprint 5 (Administrative Portals & Reporting):** Development of the Dean and Department Chair dashboards, exception alerts, and automated accreditation exports.


6. **Sprint 6 (Verification & Usability Testing):** Execution of load testing, security audits, and empirical SUS/ISO evaluations.

# Chapter III: Results and Discussions

## Project Capabilities and Operational Workflows (SOP 1)

*How can a web-based evaluation platform be architected to enable secure, high-concurrency evaluation queuing, instantaneous cryptographic anonymity decoupling, and atomic examination clearance issuance upon survey completion?*

EvaluaTech addresses this problem through coordinated browser presentation, Laravel middleware services, and an ACID-compliant MySQL database layer.

```
                 ATOMIC EVALUATION SUBMISSION PIPELINE
                 
   [POST Request: /api/evaluation/submit]
                     │
                     ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 1. Validate Form & Idempotency Key  │
  └──────────────────┬──────────────────┘
                     │
                     ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 2. Check Submission Integrity       │
  │    (Window Active, Non-Neg Elapsed) │
  └──────────────────┬──────────────────┘
                     │
                     ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 3. Execute DB Transaction (ACID Isolation)                  │
  │    - INSERT INTO `evaluation_clearances`                    │
  │        (student_id, course_id, term_id, status='CLEARED')   │
  │    - INSERT INTO `evaluation_vault`                         │
  │        (course_id, faculty_id, ratings_json, comment_hash)  │
  └──────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────────┘
                     │
                     ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 4. Issue Clearance Verification     │
  │    Token (Signed JWT Token)         │
  └──────────────────┬──────────────────┘
                     │
                     ▼
  ┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
  │ 5. Queue Background NLP Job         │
  └─────────────────────────────────────┘

```

When a student submits an evaluation:

1. The server validates that the active session matches an enrolled course section and confirms that the unique submission key has not been processed previously, preventing duplicate submissions.


2. The submission payload is checked against the active evaluation window schedule and verified for non-negative elapsed duration.
3. An atomic database transaction executes: the student's institutional record in the clearance table is marked as completed, while the rating data is saved into the decoupled vault table without student identification metadata.


4. A signed clearance verification token is issued to the client, unlocking examination clearance while ensuring survey responses remain completely anonymous.

$$\text{Polarity Score } P \in [-1.0, +1.0]$$

* **Pedagogical Delivery:** Comprehensibility, subject matter mastery, teaching pacing.
* **Assessment & Grading:** Fairness of exams, timeliness of grade returns, rubric clarity.
* **Classroom Management:** Punctuality, syllabus adherence, student engagement.
* **Professional Conduct:** Respectfulness, empathy, learning atmosphere.
